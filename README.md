# JaundiceCheck

**Smartphone screening for newborn jaundice during ASHA home visits**

*Idea submission for the [NeuraViz AI Challenge 2026-27](https://neuraviz.com/aichallenge2026/), Stage 2 (idea only).*

The public challenge FAQ says the NeuraViz team reviews each idea. The page does not publish a detailed scoring rubric or weights.

## Problem

Very high bilirubin can cause permanent neurological injury, yet bilirubin levels are hard to judge by appearance alone. India's Home-Based Newborn Care program schedules a visit by a community health worker (ASHA) on day 3, within the usual bilirubin peak window of days 3–5. See the [clinical references and claim audit](IDEA.md#references).

## AI Solution

JaundiceCheck proposes an offline Android app that combines guided skin and eye photos with the newborn's age and gestational age to flag possible need for a bilirubin blood test. A printed color reference would help calibrate photos. The tool would not diagnose jaundice or override standard referrals.

**Validation targets:** 2,400 newborns, including 800 in a held-out test; at least 95% sensitivity and 75% specificity against blood bilirubin–based referral criteria, with results broken out by skin tone, phone model, and lighting.

**Pilot safeguards:** Caregivers could decline photos without affecting care. The phone would process images without saving them by default, and a pediatric lead would review subgroup failures before any use that could change care.

## Expected Impact

The goal is to help ASHAs identify newborns who need testing sooner, without sending too many families for unnecessary testing. That benefit would need to be measured against usual care.
